      ABOUT · VAIBHAV VISHAL

      The longer version

      Six years designing things a lot of people use at once, mostly in entertainment. Here is what that has actually involved, how I work, and what I do when I am not doing it.

      I am a product designer, based in India, six-plus years in. Almost all of it consumer — products where the population using them is large enough that a decision you make on a Tuesday shows up in a chart on Friday, and where the interesting problems are rarely the ones you can see on a single screen.

      Most of my recent work has been in entertainment and content products: content discovery, streaming, subscriptions, merchandising, and the experiences that carry all of that onto a television and back onto a phone. Those are ecosystems rather than apps. The catalogue, the entitlement, the bill, the home screen and the player all have to agree with each other, and the design problem is usually that they do not.

      What I actually do

      Less of it is drawing than people expect. A good share of my week is spent on the shape of the thing — what a surface is for, what it is allowed to promise, what has to be true underneath before an interface can say it plainly. The rest is the craft: interaction, motion, hierarchy, the typography of a screen somebody looks at for half a second while doing something else.

      I have spent a lot of time on growth and experimentation, which mostly teaches you humility. You watch a change you were sure about do nothing, and a change you argued against move a number, and after enough of those you stop treating your own taste as evidence. It stays useful — it is what generates the hypotheses — but it stops being the thing that settles them.

      How I work

      • Frame before you draw. The first version of almost every brief I get is a solution wearing a problem's clothes. Working out what the actual question is buys more than any number of explorations.
      • Design against the messy account, not the demo one. The tidy user with one subscription and no history is the user nobody has. Systems get tangled because they were designed for the screenshot.
      • Say what it costs. Every decision worth making gives something up. If I cannot name what a proposal loses, I do not understand it well enough to defend it yet.
      • Ship the smallest honest version. Then learn in public, with instrumentation in place before launch rather than bolted on after somebody asks how it went.

      I work best close to engineering. Most of the projects I am proud of came out of a conversation where somebody said "we could do that, but not the way you have drawn it" and the third idea was better than either of the first two.

      Away from the screen

      I read fiction, mostly graphic novels. I used to grind tactical shooters and moved on — these days the competitive urge goes into weight training instead, logged in detail, in a gym at home. To reset I pour paint, which is calm in a way a screen never is.

      And I tinker with hardware. There is an 8TB mini server in this flat that exists for no reason beyond wanting to build it, and a keyboard I put together switch by switch. This website is the same instinct: the city behind it is eight and a half thousand lines of code that draws every pixel of a skyline from scratch, at twelve frames a second, because that is how the machines I grew up with would have done it.
