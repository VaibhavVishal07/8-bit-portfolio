    PROJECT 01 · PRODUCT DESIGN · 2025 UNDER NDA

    LIGHTS, CAMERA, BANNERS

    Driving user engagement through dynamic banner design.

    Role
    Lead Product Designer
    Timeline
    2025 · 4 months
    Team
    2 designers · 4 engineers
    Platform
    Web · iOS

    This work is under NDA, so the screens below are representative placeholders rather than the real product. The write-up is real — and I'm happy to walk through the actual screens in a call.

    Overview

    The promotional banners on the home screen were the single biggest lever on what people watched and clicked — and the slowest thing in the app to change. Merchandising ran on a handful of hand-built templates, so every campaign looked the same to everyone and took days to ship.

    The problem

    Banners were static, one-size-fits-all, and locked behind engineering. Each new campaign meant someone stitching a fresh layout by hand, so the team defaulted to safe, generic art — and the most valuable real estate in the product was quietly under-earning.

    What I did

    I broke the banner into composable blocks — art, headline, tag, and call-to-action as separate layers a merchandiser could recombine — and wrote the rules that keep any combination on-brand and legible at every size. Then I prototyped the motion and the personalization, so a banner could react to what someone had already watched, and worked with engineering to make going live a config change instead of a build.

    Screens

    Outcome

    Campaigns that took days now ship in an afternoon, and the home screen finally speaks to the person looking at it rather than to everyone at once. Tap-through on the hero slot climbed through the first two launches, and the merchandising team went from filing tickets to running their own experiments.     PROJECT 02 · DESIGN SYSTEMS · 2024 UNDER NDA

    THE SOUND OF STYLE

    A sleek, smarter music player built for seamless listening.

    Role
    Design Systems Lead
    Timeline
    2024 · 6 months
    Team
    Design + Platform
    Platform
    Web

    This work is under NDA, so the screens below are representative placeholders rather than the real product. The write-up is real — and I'm happy to walk through the actual screens in a call.

    Overview

    A music app with a deep catalogue and a player that got in the way of it. Listening should feel effortless — hands-free, glanceable, alive to what you are doing — and instead it asked for a tap at every turn.

    The problem

    The now-playing screen was a wall of equal-weight controls, and the moments that mattered — starting something, handing off to the car, finding your way back to a queue — were buried under the ones that didn't. People used a fraction of what was there and felt the rest as clutter.

    What I did

    I rebuilt the player around a clear hierarchy of intent: the art and the one gesture you reach for most, front and centre; everything else a swipe away, not a tap in your face. I designed the transitions between full-screen, mini, and lock-screen so the player felt like one object moving, not three, and tuned the type and touch targets for a screen you glance at, not read.

    Screens

    Outcome

    The player got quieter and the listening got longer. Sessions ran further without a tap, the support notes about "where did my queue go" dried up, and the pattern became the template the rest of the app was rebuilt against. (Exact figures under NDA — happy to walk through them.)

    PROJECT 03 · MOBILE · 2023 UNDER NDA

    EVERYTHING, ALL AT ONCE

    Unifying OTT subscription and viewing experience.

    Role
    Product Designer
    Timeline
    2023 · 3 months
    Team
    Solo design
    Platform
    iOS · Android

    This work is under NDA, so the screens below are representative placeholders rather than the real product. The write-up is real — and I'm happy to walk through the actual screens in a call.

    Overview

    One account, several subscriptions, and no single place that made sense of them. People were paying for tiers they had forgotten, watching across apps that did not talk to each other, and hitting a paywall with no idea which plan they were even on.

    The problem

    Subscription and viewing had grown up as separate products bolted together. The seams showed everywhere — a show you could open but not play, an upgrade that took you out of the app, a bill that never matched what you thought you had. Trust leaked out at every seam.

    What I did

    I mapped the whole tangle — every plan, entitlement, and dead end — and designed one spine that carried a viewer from "I want to watch this" to "I'm watching it" without ever leaving the story they were in. Upgrades happened inline, in context, with the price and the plan stated plainly; the home screen finally knew what you could actually play. I pressure-tested it against the messiest real accounts, not the tidy demo one.

    Screens

    Outcome

    The paywall stopped being a wall. Upgrades moved into the moment of wanting something instead of a settings screen nobody visited, fewer people bounced at the point of purchase, and "what am I even paying for" stopped being the top support question.     PROJECT 04 · BRAND · 2022 UNDER NDA

    HOW TO DECIDE?

    Easing user’s decision making to purchase a service.

    Role
    Designer
    Timeline
    2022 · 2 months
    Team
    With founders
    Platform
    Brand · Web

    This work is under NDA, so the screens below are representative placeholders rather than the real product. The write-up is real — and I'm happy to walk through the actual screens in a call.

    Overview

    A service people wanted but stalled on buying. The offer was good; the decision was hard. Too many plans, too many caveats, and a comparison page that asked you to hold a spreadsheet in your head at the exact moment you were trying to say yes.

    The problem

    Choice was being treated as a feature. Every option, edge case, and add-on was laid out with equal weight, so the person most ready to buy was the one made to work hardest — and a lot of them simply left to "think about it," which usually meant never.

    What I did

    I reframed the flow around the decision, not the catalogue: ask the two or three things that actually change the answer, then recommend one plan and explain why, with the others one tap away for the people who want them. I designed the comparison so it read as a set of trade-offs rather than a grid, and wrote the microcopy to remove doubt at each step instead of adding disclaimers.

    Screens

    Outcome

    Deciding got easier and buying followed. More people reached a plan and fewer abandoned at the compare step, "which one is right for me" stopped being a support thread, and a recommend-one-then-explain pattern carried over to the rest of the funnel. (Exact figures under NDA — happy to walk through them.)
